**Icelandic Language Self-Study Tracker** - Small but growing niche community interested in heritage language learning and sustainable tourism careers. Evidence: HelloTalk community, Samrómur pronunciation project exist for practice. Limited community size and lower exam urgency compared to candidates below. Rejected due to smallest addressable market (~50K-100K learners globally).

**Turkish Language Study Tracker** - Moderate demand from diaspora and career-motivated learners. Evidence: Duolingo, Babbel, Taalhammer, Preply all serve this market; LanguaTracker and Notion templates available for tracking. Established competition in both teaching platforms and tracking tools makes differentiation harder.

**Swahili Language Learning Tracker** - Strong economic motivation (Africa's growth) and diaspora learner base (150-200M native speakers). Evidence: Duolingo, SwahiliPod101, general language trackers (LingoTrack, PolyLogger) exist; university courses offered; no Swahili-specific tracker found. **Biblical/Classical Greek (Ecclesiastical) Study Tracker** - Niche religious learner community with specific textual focus. Evidence: Greek For All, Biblingo, Accordance, Bill Mounce platforms with built-in tracking. Existing platforms already consolidate this niche; community is smaller than language learners focused on speaking proficiency. Rejected due to competition from established platforms.

**Irish Gaelic (Gaeilge) Self-Study Progress Tracker** - **SELECTED.** Massive documented learner base: 1.87M people in Republic of Ireland claim Irish proficiency; significant diaspora communities globally (UK, US, Australia, Canada) driven by heritage/cultural reconnection. Evidence: UK Irish enrollment increased 155% over 5 years; 120M+ #Gaeilge TikTok views showing cultural interest; Teastas Eorpach na Gaeilge (TEG) certification exam offers formal pathway (A1-C1, held twice yearly, 2,000+ certifications since 2006, several hundred yearly). CRITICALLY: No Irish-specific study tracker products exist (Gumroad, Etsy, Notion searches returned only generic language templates, no Gaeilge-specific products). Gaeilgeoir is a full platform/app, not a spreadsheet template. Self-study tracking needs are significant across all segments: casual heritage learners, exam-focused learners, diaspora reconnectors — study hours, vocabulary acquisition, grammar pattern coverage (Irish mutations), skills practice logging (listening/speaking/writing/reading), optional exam-tracking for TEG registrants. Clear under-service compared to JLPT or other mainstream languages.

## Why this niche

Irish Gaelic is a **genuinely underserved niche with large documented demand and zero dedicated tracking competition**, making it substantially stronger than the initially rejected JLPT niche. 

**Addressable Market (Honest Assessment):**
- **1.87M+ people in Republic of Ireland** claim Irish proficiency (primary market)
- **Significant diaspora globally**: UK (+155% Irish enrollment over 5 years), US, Australia, Canada, heritage learners reconnecting with cultural roots
- **Cultural momentum**: 120M+ #Gaeilge TikTok views, ongoing Irish-language revival movement, political/cultural support for the language
- **Exam pathway**: Teastas Eorpach na Gaeilge (TEG) provides formal certification (A1-C1), held twice yearly, 2,000+ certifications since 2006, several hundred yearly — but this is ONE use case, not the entire market
- **Broader segments**: Casual heritage learners (largest), diaspora reconnectors, exam registrants (100-200/year), parents teaching children, cultural enthusiasts

The niche is NOT "TEG exam takers only" (~100-200 people/year) — it's **all self-taught Irish learners across skill levels and motivations**, where TEG exam prep is one supported use case.

**Competition Reality (Honest Assessment):**
- NO Irish-specific study tracker products exist for the general self-study population (verified: Gumroad, Etsy, Notion marketplace — only generic language templates found)
- Unlike JLPT (served by free GyanMirai, JLPTBooks, multiple Notion templates, Etsy products), Irish Gaelic has zero dedicated tracker ecosystem
- Gaeilgeoir is a full platform/app (gamified lessons), not a spreadsheet template for self-study tracking
- General language-learning templates exist but don't address Irish-specific complexity (VSO word order, mutations, CEFR alignment for Irish learners)
- Existing Irish platforms (Gael Linn, Oideas Gael, Gaelchultúr) focus on instruction, not tracking infrastructure

**Why This Niche Survives Honest Scrutiny:**
1. **Large, diverse TAM**: 1.87M+ potential users (not 100-200 exam registrants) across self-study, heritage, diaspora, and exam segments — sustainable for long-tail product revenue
2. **Zero existing competition**: No tracker template or dedicated product captures this market (unlike JLPT, Turkish, Swahili which have options)
3. **Multiple use cases, one product**: General study tracking serves casual learners + diaspora reconnectors; TEG exam fields serve registrants; same template works for all motivations
4. **Emotional stickiness**: Heritage/cultural reconnection is a strong motivator beyond skill acquisition; learners tend to revisit and practice long-term
5. **Content funnel potential**: Blog content resonates across segments — "reconnecting with heritage," "Irish language journey," "family storytelling in Irish," "TEG exam strategy" — funnels to tracker product
6. **Product-market fit**: Tracker addresses real tracking needs (study consistency, vocabulary acquisition, grammar pattern coverage, skill balance) that general templates don't tailor for Irish learners

**Comparison to Niche A (Multi-Cat Tracker):**
- A: Specific pain point (multi-cat litter tracking) + large market (millions of multi-cat households) + emerging competition (PurrTrack) = proceeded
- C: Specific pain point (self-study tracking for heritage learners) + large market (1.87M+ Irish speakers + diaspora) + ZERO competition = stronger case
