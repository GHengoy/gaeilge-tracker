The TEG Exam: Structure and Stakes

The Teastas Eorpach na Gaeilge (TEG, or European Qualification in Irish) is a formal certification of Irish language proficiency recognized across Europe. It's offered at six levels aligned to the CEFR (Common European Framework of Reference): A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, and C2. The exam is held twice yearly and includes separate components for listening, reading, writing, and speaking. For learners serious about demonstrating proficiency in Irish, TEG certification carries weight in employment, education, and cultural contexts.

If you're planning to sit the TEG exam, you face a different study challenge than casual learners. You need to know not just that you're learning Irish, but specifically whether you're on track to reach your target level by exam day. You need to know which components are your weakness and demand focused attention. You need to see your mock exam scores trending upward, not downward. And as the exam date approaches, you need to manage intensity strategically.

The Problem: Guesswork About Readiness

Without tracking, TEG prep becomes an anxiety-filled guessing game. You study for weeks, thinking "I should be ready," but you have no objective measure of whether you actually are. You take a mock exam and score 65%, but you don't know whether that's trending up or down compared to last month. You don't know which sections to focus on in your final weeks. You can't separate what you've actually learned from what you think you've learned.

This uncertainty drives inefficient study decisions. You might over-prepare areas where you're already strong, wasting precious time. You might under-prepare weak areas because they feel too hard. You might repeat the same study material for months because you're not tracking progress and don't realize you've already mastered it. All the while, your exam date is approaching and your actual readiness level remains invisible.

Using the Tracker to Manage TEG Prep

A tracker transforms TEG prep from guesswork to evidence-based preparation. Here's how the exam-specific fields work together:

  • Target Level and Target Section: Knowing your target level (A1-C2) and which exam component you're focusing on that day shapes what you study. If you're targeting B1 and your weak area is writing, you might spend focused time on past tense formation and complex sentence construction. Logging this daily reminds you of your specific goal and prevents aimless studying.
  • Mock Exam Scores: As you work through past papers or practice exams, log your scores. Over time, you'll see a trend. Are you improving? Plateauing? Declining? This data is invaluable. An upward trend means your study approach is working; keep doing it. A plateau means you need to change something. A declining trend is an alarm bell that demands immediate attention.
  • Days to Exam: This column keeps the countdown real. When it says "120 days to exam," you're in study-building phase. You can experiment, take risks with new materials, and consolidate foundational skills. When it says "30 days to exam," you shift to high-intensity practice and mock exams. When it says "5 days to exam," you're in confidence-maintenance mode, reviewing weak areas but not learning new material.
  • Target Completion Percentage: Set a realistic target (70% of marks for A1-A2, 75% for B1-B2, 80% for C1, 85% for C2). Use your mock exam scores to refine this as you approach the exam date. Your tracker becomes a progress bar: "I scored 60% on last month's mock, my target is 75%, I have eight weeks to close that gap."

Sample TEG Prep Timeline

14-16 weeks before exam (study-building phase): Target comprehensive coverage of your level's vocabulary, grammar, and exam format. Aim for 8-10 hours of study per week across all components. Take your first full mock exam around week 12.

8-10 weeks before exam (skill-focus phase): Use your mock exam score to identify weak sections. If your listening was 65% but your reading was 80%, dedicate more time to listening. Take mock exams every 2 weeks. Aim for 10-12 hours per week, with increasing intensity on weak areas.

4-6 weeks before exam (practice-heavy phase): Take mock exams weekly or twice weekly. Use the feedback from each mock to focus your study. Aim for 12-15 hours per week. At this point, you should be taking full, timed exam simulations and scoring them like real exams.

2-3 weeks before exam (refinement phase): Continue mock exams, but reduce their frequency slightly to avoid burnout. Focus on weak areas identified in previous mocks. Aim for 10-12 hours per week. At this point, most of your study should feel like review, not learning new material.

Final week (confidence maintenance): Review problem areas, but don't try to learn new material. Take one final mock exam to build confidence. Spend 5-8 hours reviewing, not stressing. Trust the work you've done.

Real-Time Adjustments Based on Tracker Data

The tracker's power lies in its ability to inform adjustments. If you notice that your mock exam scores are stalling at 60% when your target is 75%, and you have eight weeks left, you need to make changes now. Maybe your study hours are too low. Maybe you're not spending enough time on writing (the most difficult component for many learners). Maybe you're avoiding the weaker skills and need to force yourself to practice them more.

The tracker makes these decisions concrete. Instead of vague feelings about progress ("I feel like I should be ready"), you have data. "My last three mocks were 58%, 60%, 59%. My trend is flat. I need to increase study hours from 8/week to 12/week and spend more time on speaking practice." That's a decision you can act on immediately.

Psychological Benefits of Tracking

Beyond the practical benefits, tracking your TEG prep has psychological value. Exam anxiety is partly rooted in uncertainty. The more concretely you know where you stand and what you need to do, the less anxious you feel. A tracker that shows your mock exam scores trending from 55% to 62% to 68% doesn't just give you data; it gives you confidence. You can see your actual progress accumulating week by week.
