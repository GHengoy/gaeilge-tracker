The TEG Exam: Structure and Stakes

The Teastas Eorpach na Gaeilge (TEG, or European Qualification in Irish) is a formal certification of Irish language proficiency recognized across Europe.

  • Levels: six, aligned to the CEFR (Common European Framework of Reference): A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, and C2.
  • Timing: the exam is held twice yearly.
  • Components: separate sections for listening, reading, writing, and speaking.
  • Value: for learners serious about demonstrating proficiency in Irish, TEG certification carries weight in employment, education, and cultural contexts.

If you're planning to sit the TEG exam, you face a different study challenge than casual learners. You need to know:

  • Not just that you're learning Irish, but whether you're on track to reach your target level by exam day.
  • Which components are your weakness and demand focused attention.
  • That your mock exam scores are trending upward, not downward.

And as the exam date approaches, you need to manage intensity strategically.

The Problem: Guesswork About Readiness

Without tracking, TEG prep becomes an anxiety-filled guessing game. You study for weeks, thinking "I should be ready," but you have no objective measure of whether you actually are.

You take a mock exam and score 65%, but you don't know whether that's trending up or down compared to last month. You don't know which sections to focus on in your final weeks. You can't separate what you've actually learned from what you think you've learned.

How uncertainty wastes study time

  • You might over-prepare areas where you're already strong, wasting precious time.
  • You might under-prepare weak areas because they feel too hard.
  • You might repeat the same study material for months because you don't realize you've already mastered it.

All the while, your exam date is approaching and your actual readiness level remains invisible.

Using the Tracker to Manage TEG Prep

A tracker transforms TEG prep from guesswork to evidence-based preparation. Here's how the exam-specific fields work together:

  • Target Level and Target Section. Knowing your target level (A1-C2) and which exam component you're focusing on that day shapes what you study. If you're targeting B1 and your weak area is writing, you might spend focused time on past tense formation and complex sentence construction. Logging this daily reminds you of your specific goal and prevents aimless studying.
  • Mock Exam Scores. As you work through past papers or practice exams, log your scores. Over time, you'll see a trend, and this data is invaluable.
  • Days to Exam. This column keeps the countdown real and tells you which mode you should be in.
  • Target Completion Percentage. Set a realistic target and use your mock exam scores to refine it as you approach the exam date.

Real-Time Adjustments Based on Tracker Data

The tracker's power lies in its ability to inform adjustments. If your mock exam scores are stalling at 60% when your target is 75%, and you have eight weeks left, you need to make changes now. Possible causes:

  • Your study hours are too low.
  • You're not spending enough time on writing (the most difficult component for many learners).
  • You're avoiding the weaker skills and need to force yourself to practice them more.

The tracker makes these decisions concrete. Instead of vague feelings about progress ("I feel like I should be ready"), you have data.

A decision you can act on

"My last three mocks were 58%, 60%, 59%. My trend is flat. I need to increase study hours from 8/week to 12/week and spend more time on speaking practice."

Psychological Benefits of Tracking

Beyond the practical benefits, tracking your TEG prep has psychological value. Exam anxiety is partly rooted in uncertainty. The more concretely you know where you stand and what you need to do, the less anxious you feel.

A tracker that shows your mock exam scores trending from 55% to 62% to 68% doesn't just give you data; it gives you confidence. You can see your actual progress accumulating week by week.
